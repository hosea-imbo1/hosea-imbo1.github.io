Research

My research focuses on model-based deep learning for computational imaging and inverse problems. I formulate imaging tasks as regularized inverse problems, select suitable optimization algorithms, and unroll their iterative updates into trainable neural architectures. This allows known components of the image-formation model and physical structure of the problem to be explicitly incorporated into the network, rather than learned entirely from data.

The goal is to develop reconstruction methods that combine the interpretability and structure of classical optimisation with the representational power of deep learning, while reducing computational complexity and training-data requirements.

Current focus: I investigate indirect Time-of-Flight (iToF) depth reconstruction under scattering, formulated as an inverse deconvolution problem. Using complex-valued I/Q measurements and an explicit model of the scattering point-spread function, I aim to recover accurate depth information from degraded measurements.
